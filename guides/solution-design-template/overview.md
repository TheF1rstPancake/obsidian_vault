# Guide: The solution design template

A fill-in-the-blank solution design document in three parts: **problem definition** (solution-agnostic), a **requirements table** written without a single product name, and only *then* the **mechanics of your specific solution**. That sequence is the whole point. Get Parts 1 and 2 honest before your product shows up, and you've built an artifact a customer could take to any vendor — which is exactly why it still holds up at implementation, expansion, and renewal long after the deal closes.

For SEs, SCs, and anyone who inherits an account and has to reconstruct what was actually promised.

## What's inside

- **Part 1 — Problem definition** — the four boxes (current state, problems, goals, ideal solution), all in the customer's world, none in your product's
- **Part 2 — Solution-agnostic requirements** — a need/nice-to-have table any vendor could be scored against, with rationale tracing each requirement back to a goal
- **Part 3 — Solution recommendation** — your bridge, a requirements-to-solution mapping with explicit out-of-scope items, implementation, and testable acceptance criteria
- **Open items** — the honest list of what isn't resolved yet
