# Forward Deployed Engineers are consultants with a better title

## The thesis

The "Forward Deployed Engineer" title is having a moment. Strip the branding and the role looks a lot like consulting: client-embedded, scoped engagements, your output measured by the customer's outcome rather than internal product wins. That's not a knock — consulting is a real craft — but I have a problem with the term, and the problem is that I can't tell what it's actually doing.

Is it marketing? Is it a way to attract talent? Does it present something to customers that is meaningfully different? Because plenty of people have already pointed this out: forward deployed engineering is functionally consulting and professional services under a different name. The usual rebuttal is "no, no, we don't charge for our forward deployed engineering." But you don't have to charge for professional services. Not charging is *one* of the defining characteristics of how the work gets packaged — it doesn't make it a new category. Implementation engineers, onboarding teams, professional services — these have existed forever. This is not a new concept.

So I'm left wondering whether the title is mostly a recruiting instrument. Integration talent is genuinely hard to find: you need people who are technically savvy but still customer-facing, who are real engineers — or want to be — and who have presence in front of a customer. That cohort is rare. So are we just marketing to them because "forward deployed engineer" sounds sexier than "implementation engineer," and that's the title they want on LinkedIn? Or is the role actually materially different? I don't think it is. But let's leave it open.
