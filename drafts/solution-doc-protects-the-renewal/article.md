# The solution design document protects the renewal, not the sale

*This piece assumes the four-boxes-plus-bridge structure from [Are You Trying to Be Right, or Help the Customer?](/are-you-trying-to-be-right-or-help-the-customer/); the worked template lives in the [Solution Design Template guide](/guide-solution-design-template/).*

If a document is written to get a signature, nobody revisits it once the signature lands. That's how the solution design document ends up treated as a closing artifact: it gets the deal signed, then it gets archived. The document's biggest payoff is 9–18 months later, when an implementation team has handed off to CSM, the original AE has rotated to another segment, and someone is asking "why does this customer keep raising tickets about X?" or "why won't they renew?"

If the document is good, you have an answer. If it isn't, the whole post-sale org is reconstructing the customer's expectations from Slack threads and a few stale email chains.

The closing lifetime of the document is a few weeks. The post-sale lifetime is years.

The reason the document can do that job is the same structure already argued in the right-or-help piece and built out in the guide: customer problem first, solution-agnostic requirements as the shared agreement, vendor recommendation last. The solution-agnostic requirements section is what both parties can still point at a year later. Verbal commitments don't travel that far.

Write the version CSM will need in eighteen months, not just the one that gets the AE to close. It's usually the same document. It just needs someone to remember, while writing it, that the reader on the other end of the timeline hasn't been hired yet.
