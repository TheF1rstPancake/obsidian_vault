The first pancake is the one you throw away. Every cook knows this. The pan isn't hot enough, the batter hasn't settled, you haven't found the right timing yet. You flip it too early, or too late, and it comes out wrong. You don't serve it. You make it anyway, because you have to — that's how you learn the pan.

That's the frame for most of what I've figured out about working.

I'm Giovanni. I've spent my career in technical sales and solutions engineering — presales, implementation, a few pivots in between. I'm now in leadership, building and running SE teams. Most of what I write here comes from things I got wrong before I got right: deals I read incorrectly, hires I misjudged, frameworks I picked up from someone smarter and then had to unlearn when the context changed.

The topics cluster around a few questions I keep coming back to:

- How do you hire for judgment — not credentials, not logos — when judgment is so hard to define?
- How do you build teams that can operate without constant supervision, in environments where the work is ambiguous by default?
- What does good actually look like in a customer-facing technical role, and how do you recognize it before it shows up in the numbers?

If you work in sales engineering or solutions consulting, a lot of the examples will be familiar. But the underlying questions — about judgment, about how teams work, about what makes a decision good — aren't specific to any function. They show up anywhere the work is gray-area by default and there's no flowchart yet.

I write when I have something to say. Subscribe if you want to hear it.
