Anthropic just posted a job listing for what reads as a Claude Evangelist targeting startups. The hiring manager describes wanting someone who can hop on stage, hype people up, and run hackathons to get developers excited about building on Claude.

On the surface, it's a standard devrel hire. But I think it betrays something more interesting about where Anthropic — and the AI market — actually is right now.

## The tool that wins is the one you can start with fastest

Most SaaS businesses operating in new territory eventually figure this out: customers pick the tool they can get up and running the fastest, not the tool that is academically correct for the job.

If a user can make progress toward their objective with you, and they can do it quickly, they're more likely to stay. Often they don't even know what the end state is. They just start, and keep going, and keep going.

That early traction compounds. It's so much harder to move off a tool once you've started using it than it is to start from scratch in the first place.

The big cloud providers have been running this playbook for years. AWS throws money at startups so they're the first tool you integrate with, and then the niceties and the weird AWS-specific bits make switching to Azure — even when Azure is cheaper — too painful. Azure plays the other side: race to the bottom on price, get people locked in, then keep charging.

## Category definition

I had the chance to work with the team at Play Bigger, who wrote the book on category design. A lot of what they talk about is that you want to be the first one to *define* the category, because once users engage with your definition, you become top of mind.

Uber is the canonical example. Even if you call a Lyft, you don't say you're "catching a Lyft" — you say you're "calling an Uber." You might still choose Lyft when Uber's surge pricing is bad, but Uber owns the verb. DoorDash owns "I'm gonna DoorDash something." Kleenex owns single-use tissue paper.

This is what an evangelist hire is really about: owning the definition of how you do AI in your startup.
