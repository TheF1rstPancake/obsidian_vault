# How to actually evaluate judgement in early hires

> Stub. Seed thoughts captured from a brain-dump conversation on 2026-05-07. Awaiting voice recordings to flesh out.

## The setup

"They have good judgement." / "I want them to have good judgement." This phrase comes up constantly in early-stage hiring, but it's almost never defined. The people who have it can't articulate it. The people who don't have it never try, because it's not a skillset they recognize as one.

## The thesis

Good judgement, in practice, is the intersection of three things:

1. **Prioritization** — knowing what to do *now* vs *later* vs *never*, especially under ambiguity
2. **Motivation** — understanding *why* something matters, to whom, and whether the effort is justified
3. **Technical skill** — having the chops to execute on the choice once it's made

Strip out any one of the three and "judgement" collapses. [?]
