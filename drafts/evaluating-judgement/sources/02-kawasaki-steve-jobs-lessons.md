## Key claims

- **Core principle**: "Assemble a team of talents greater than your own. A-players attract A-players."
- **The bozo explosion**: "You stagnate with mediocrity if B players recruit C players." B players hire C players to feel superior; C players hire D players. One day you wake up surrounded by bozos.
- The dynamic is recursive: the standard for "A-player" is "someone who attracts other A-players," with operational definition left to the reader.
- One tangential mention of mindset: "bozosity — a contagious skepticism suggesting greatness can't be achieved." This is about attitude/posture, not capability.

## My notes

Guy's article distills "better than someone else" to mean purely technically. Someone with good technical skills can still suck at figuring out what to work on and can be unmotivated to actually do the work. The piece is missing the multi-faceted nature of what it means to be "good." It's also 15-20 years old — written before the modern reality that someone's technical skill can be augmented by AI, which makes motivation and prioritization *more* differentiating, not less. The article should engage with this as a "yes, and" — the bozo explosion is real, but the way the original framing measured "A-player" is one reason the explosion keeps happening even at companies that try to follow the rule.
