## Key claims

- The concept is attributed to **Guy Kawasaki** (who worked with Jobs), not Jobs directly. Worth correcting in the article — currently the framing implies it comes from Jobs himself.
- The talent-hierarchy formulation: "A players hire A players. If you're an A player, you want to surround yourself with people as smart or smarter than you."
- The inverse dynamic, more granularly stated than our current article: "7s and 8s want to be around 5s and 6s in order to feel superior."
- The tactical implication for hiring managers: **"Wait longer to have the right person on their team than settle for a B or C player."**
- An adjacent move on leader self-awareness: leaders need "a rare blend of confidence and humility" to actively seek people who push them to be better — i.e., the article's "people who have all three pillars are usually terrible at teaching" failure mode is partly an ego problem, not just a decomposition problem.

## Where this might land in the article

- **"The bozo explosion" section**: correct the attribution (Kawasaki coined it, talking about Jobs's hiring philosophy) — small but matters for credibility.
- **Same section**: the 1-10 scale ("7s and 8s want to be around 5s and 6s") is a sharper handle than our current "two-of-three hires hire one-of-three" framing. Consider adopting it alongside, or using it to make the gradient more vivid.
- **New paragraph after the bozo explosion section**: introduce the "wait longer" tactical principle. This is the kind of steal-able advice that aligns with our writing principle. Possible structure: "The defense is mechanical, not magical: wait longer than feels comfortable, and resist the pressure to fill the seat."
- **"What this looks like on a manager" section**: the "confidence + humility to hire above your level" idea is a clean addition to the manager's job framing. Currently we say a manager's job is to fill the gaps; the inverse — actively recruiting people who fill *your* gaps — is the missing complement.
